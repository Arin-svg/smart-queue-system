                                             ABSTRACT

Queue management is an important aspect of healthcare services as it affects patient
waiting time, operational efficiency, and patient satisfaction. Existing queue management
processes in university health centres often lack real-time queue tracking, accurate
waiting time estimation, and intelligent decision-support features, leading to uncertainty
for patients and challenges in managing patient flow.
This project proposes the development of a Smart Queue System for University Health
Centre (SQS) to improve queue management in university health centre environments.
Klinik Kesihatan Peramu Jaya, Pekan, Pahang is used as the case study for requirement
gathering and system validation. The proposed system provides QR code-based queue
registration, appointment booking, real-time queue monitoring, smart notification
updates, and administrative reporting. It also integrates AI-based Dynamic Waiting Time
Prediction and Intelligent Queue Priority Recommendation to support more efficient
queue management.
The system will be developed using the Waterfall Software Development Life Cycle
(SDLC), adopting the Model­View­Controller (MVC) architecture with Flutter, Laravel
RESTful API, and MySQL. The completed system will be evaluated through Functional
Testing and User Acceptance Testing (UAT). The expected outcome is an intelligent and
user-friendly queue management system that improves queue transparency, enhances
patient experience, and supports more efficient healthcare service delivery in university
health centre environments.

                                         INTRODUCTION

1.1 Introduction

         Queue management is an important part of healthcare services because it affects
     how patients wait, how staff manage patient flow, and how smoothly daily clinic
     operations run. In a university health centre, students and staff usually visit the clinic
     for consultation, treatment, medical check-ups, or other health-related services.
     When the number of patients increases, especially during busy hours, a manual queue
     system can become difficult to manage. According to recent healthcare digitalization
     trends, the use of computerized queue systems can reduce waiting time, improve
     patient experience, and enhance staff productivity in healthcare environments.

         Currently, many clinics still depend on traditional queue methods such as
     physical number tickets, handwritten records, or verbal calling at the counter.
     Although this method is simple, it can cause several problems. Patients may not know
     how long they need to wait, what their current queue position is, or when their turn
     is near. This can lead to confusion, overcrowding in the waiting area, and
     dissatisfaction among patients.

         Recent advances in mobile computing, cloud technologies, and artificial
     intelligence (AI) have enabled the development of intelligent healthcare information
     systems capable of supporting operational decision-making in addition to automating
     routine administrative tasks. Rather than functioning solely as a queue numbering
     application, a smart queue management system can analyse real-time operational
     data to estimate waiting time, support queue prioritisation, and provide dynamic
     notifications that improve communication between patients and healthcare providers.

         Therefore, this project proposes a development of a Smart Queue System for
     University Health Centre (SQS). The proposed system aims to enhance the digital
     queue management by integrating intelligent decision-support capabilities, including

                                                         14
     AI-based waiting time prediction, intelligent queue priority recommendation for staff
     review, and dynamic notification services. These features are intended to improve
     queue transparency, reduce patient uncertainty, and assist staffs in managing patient
     flow more effectively while ensuring that all clinical decisions remain under the
     authority of authorised healthcare professionals.

         Although the proposed system is designed for university health centre
     environments, this project adopts Klinik Kesihatan Peramu Jaya, Pekan, Pahang as
     the case study for requirement gathering, workflow analysis, and system validation.
     The clinic was selected because its operational processes including patient
     registration, consultation services, pharmacy services, and outpatient queue
     management are closely resembled in university health centres. Using a real
     healthcare environment enables the proposed system to be developed based on actual
     operational requirements while ensuring that the resulting solution remains
     applicable to university health centre settings.

         The system will be developed using Flutter as the mobile application framework,
     Laravel as the backend RESTful API, and MySQL as the database management
     system within a three-tier architecture. Through the integration of intelligent
     decision-support capabilities and modern mobile technologies, the Smart Queue
     System is expected to improve healthcare service efficiency, enhance patient
     experience, and support more effective queue management within university health
     centre environments.

1.2 Problem Statement

              Efficient queue management is essential for ensuring the quality of healthcare
     services, as it directly influences patient satisfaction, operational efficiency, and the
     overall healthcare experience. Although many healthcare facilities have adopted
     digital technologies to support patient registration and appointment management,
     queue management remains a significant challenge, particularly during peak
     operating hours. Patients frequently experience uncertainty while waiting for
     consultation because existing queue processes provide limited information regarding

                                                         15
queue progress and expected waiting time. This lack of transparency often leads to
repeated enquiries at registration counters, increased waiting area congestion, and
reduced patient satisfaction.

   Another challenge is the limited capability of conventional queue management
systems to assist healthcare staff in monitoring and coordinating patient flow
efficiently. Registration staff are often required to manually monitor queue
progression, consultation room availability, and patient movement throughout the
consultation process. As the number of patients increases, manual coordination
becomes more difficult, potentially affecting operational efficiency and delaying
service delivery. Furthermore, administrators have limited access to analytical
information that could support service planning, queue performance evaluation, and
resource allocation.

   In addition, many existing queue management systems primarily focus on
generating queue numbers without providing intelligent features that enhance
decision-making or improve the patient experience. Most systems do not estimate
waiting time based on actual service conditions, making it difficult for patients to
plan their waiting period effectively. The absence of intelligent waiting time
prediction reduces the usefulness of digital queue systems, as patients continue to
experience uncertainty despite using electronic queue management.

   To address these issues, this project proposes the development of a Smart Queue
System for University Health Centre (SQS). The proposed system integrates QR
code-based registration, appointment scheduling, real-time queue monitoring,
automated notifications, and Dynamic Waiting Time Prediction (AI-based) to
improve queue visibility and operational efficiency. Although the proposed solution
is intended for university health centre environments, Klinik Kesihatan Peramu Jaya,
Pekan, Pahang, is selected as the case study for requirement gathering, workflow
analysis, and system validation. The proposed system is expected to provide a more
transparent, efficient, and patient-centred queue management solution that can be
adapted for university health centre environments.

2.1 Introduction

         This chapter reviews existing queue management systems, healthcare
applications, and related technologies that are relevant to the development of the
proposed Smart Queue System for University Health Centre (SQS). The purpose of this
review is to examine the features, strengths, and limitations of existing systems in order
to identify suitable approaches and determine opportunities for improvement in the
proposed system.

         Since the proposed SQS is developed as a mobile application using the Flutter
framework with Laravel API and MySQL database, this review focuses on digital queue
management systems and healthcare applications that support functionalities such as
digital queue registration, appointment booking, real-time queue monitoring, notification
services, and queue management. The selected systems also provide valuable references
for designing the user interface, system workflow, and queue management processes.

         Four existing systems have been selected for comparison: JPJeQ, Queue
Management System (QMS), Qmed Portal and Waitwhile. These systems were selected
because they represent different approaches to digital queue management across
government agencies, healthcare institutions, and commercial service organisations.
Each system provides useful features and implementation strategies that can be analysed
and adapted for the proposed Smart Queue System.

         The comparison of these systems helps identify the strengths and limitations of
current queue management solutions. Although the reviewed systems provide useful
functionalities such as digital queue registration, appointment scheduling, real-time
queue tracking, notification services, and queue analytics, they do not fully support the
operational requirements of a university health centre. In particular, existing systems

                                                         21
generally lack the integration of AI-based waiting time prediction, intelligent queue
priority recommendation, and a comprehensive healthcare workflow within a single
mobile application. These identified gaps provide the motivation for developing the
proposed Smart Queue System for University Health Centre (SQS).

2.2 Existing Systems
         In order to develop a functional and user-friendly Smart Queue System for

University Health Centre (SQS), it is important to review existing queue management
systems that provide services related to digital queue management, appointment
scheduling, real-time queue monitoring, and customer flow management. Studying
existing systems helps identify useful features, user interface designs, and system
workflows that can be adapted and improved in the proposed system.

         Four existing systems have been selected for this review, which are JPJeQ, Queue
Management System (QMS), Qmed Portal and Waitwhile. These systems were selected
because they represent different implementations of digital queue management across
government agencies, healthcare institutions, and commercial organisations. Each system
offers unique features that provide valuable insights into queue management, user
experience, and service efficiency.

         The review of these systems also helps identify the limitations of current queue
management solutions. The findings obtained from this review will be used as references
in designing the proposed Smart Queue System for University Health Centre (SQS) by
incorporating suitable features while addressing the limitations identified in the existing
systems.

waiting time at service counters and provides a more organised queue management
process.

progress and estimate when they should arrive at the selected branch.

3.4.1 System Architecture
The proposed Smart Queue System (SQS) adopts the Model­View­Controller (MVC)
architecture to separate the user interface, business logic, and data management into

                                                         56
independent components. This architecture improves maintainability, scalability, and
code reusability while supporting the Flutter frontend and Laravel backend.

              Figure 3. 4 Model­View­Controller (MVC) architecture in SQS
         Figure 3.4 shows the Model­View­Controller (MVC) architecture by the
proposed Smart Queue System for University Health Centre (SQS). The architecture
separates the application into five main components, namely config, pages, controller,
model, and services, to improve modularity, maintainability, and scalability. The
application is developed using Flutter, where the main.dart file serves as the entry point
that initializes the application and coordinates communication among the different
components.
         The config package stores the application's configuration files, including API
endpoints, authentication settings, and application configuration. These files provide the
necessary configuration for communication between the Flutter application and the
Laravel RESTful API.

                                                         57
         The pages package represents the View layer of the MVC architecture. It contains
all user interface screens for the four user roles: Patient, Registration Staff, Doctor, and
PKU Administrator. These pages include login, queue management, appointment
booking, consultation management, user management, report generation, and profile
management interfaces. The pages receive user input and display information but do not
contain business logic.

         The controller package represents the Controller layer and contains the business
logic of the application. Each controller processes user requests, validates input,
coordinates system operations, and communicates with the model and services. In
addition to the core controllers such as authentication, queue, appointment, and
consultation, the architecture also includes the AI Waiting Time Controller and AI
Priority Controller, which support the intelligent waiting time prediction and queue
priority recommendation features proposed in this project.

         The model package represents the Model layer and contains the data entities used
by the system. These entities correspond to the database tables designed in the Entity
Relationship Diagram (ERD), including User, Role, Queue, Appointment, Consultation,
Department, Room, and Notification. The models define the data structure used
throughout the application.

         The services package manages communication with external services,
particularly the Laravel RESTful API. It handles API requests, user authentication, and
notification services, enabling secure data exchange between the Flutter application and
the backend server.

         Overall, the MVC architecture separates the presentation layer, business logic,
data model, and service communication into independent components. This improves
code organization, simplifies future maintenance and feature enhancement, and supports
the implementation of intelligent queue management features within the Smart Queue
System for University Health Centre (SQS)

3.8 Potential Use of Proposal Solution

         The proposed Smart Queue System for University Health Centre (SQS) has the
potential to improve the current patient registration and queue management process at the
UMPSA University Health Centre. Currently, patients register for a queue by scanning a
QR code at the health centre entrance and completing a registration form. Although this
process enables staff to receive patient registration information, it does not provide
patients with a dedicated application to monitor their queue status, estimated waiting
time, or receive real-time notifications regarding their consultation progress.

         For patients, the proposed system provides a mobile application that enables them
to scan a QR code to register for a queue, book appointments, view queue information,
and receive Smart Notification Updates throughout the consultation process. In addition,
patients can view their AI-based estimated waiting time, allowing them to better manage
their waiting time and reduce uncertainty while waiting for consultation.

                                                        129
         For Registration Staff, the proposed system supports patient verification, queue
monitoring, and patient assignment through a centralized dashboard. The system also
provides Dynamic Waiting Time Prediction (AI-based) and Intelligent Queue Priority
Recommendation (AI-based) as decision-support features to assist Registration Staff in
managing patient flow more efficiently. However, the final assignment decision remains
under the responsibility of the Registration Staff.

         For doctors, the proposed system enables them to view assigned patients, call the
next patient, and update consultation status throughout the consultation process. The
consultation status is updated in real time, allowing queue information to remain accurate
while Smart Notification Updates are automatically sent to patients when their queue
status changes.

         For Administrators, the proposed system provides facilities for managing user
accounts, departments, consultation rooms, and system reports. The administrator can
monitor important operational information, including the total number of queues, active
queues, completed consultations, average waiting time, and department-based queue
statistics. These functions support effective system administration and assist in
monitoring the overall performance of the queue management process.

         Overall, the proposed Smart Queue System has the potential to enhance the
existing QR code-based registration process by integrating queue number generation,
real-time queue monitoring, appointment booking, AI-based waiting time prediction,
intelligent queue priority recommendations, Smart Notification Updates, and
comprehensive administrative functions. By digitalising and improving the queue
management workflow, the system is expected to reduce manual processes, improve
communication between patients and healthcare staff, and enhance the overall efficiency
of healthcare services at the University Health Centre.

4.1 Introduction

         In conclusion, the proposed Smart Queue System for University Health Centre
(SQS) aims to improve the current queue management process at the UMPSA University
Health Centre by providing a more systematic and efficient digital solution. The proposed
system enables patients to register for a queue using QR code, book appointments,
monitor queue status, receive Smart Notification Updates, and view AI-based estimated
waiting time through a mobile application.

         The proposed system supports four main user roles, namely Patient, Registration
Staff, Doctor, and Administrator. Registration Staff can verify patient registration,
monitor queues, assign patients to doctors or consultation rooms, and utilize AI-based
waiting time prediction and queue priority recommendations to support queue
management. Doctors can manage consultations by updating patient status throughout
the consultation process, while Administrators can manage users, departments,
consultation rooms, and system reports. The system is proposed to be developed using
Flutter, Laravel API, and MySQL, with the overall design supported by UML diagrams,
database design, prototype development, and testing plans.

         Finally, the proposed Smart Queue System will be evaluated using Functional
Testing and User Acceptance Testing (UAT) to ensure that the developed system meets
the specified requirements and user needs. Overall, the proposed solution is expected to
improve operational efficiency, enhance communication between patients and healthcare
staff, and provide a more transparent, convenient, and intelligent queue management
system for the University Health Centre
